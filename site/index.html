Unplug once, and the order is gone

Disconnect a display and macOS moves every Space to the laptop. Connect it again and nothing goes back: full screen apps stay on the wrong screen or land at the end of the bar, and you drag them around in Mission Control by hand.

SpaceDock puts it back

It saves the order of your Spaces for each set of monitors, like “Laptop only” or “Laptop and DELL”. When the displays settle, it restores that order on its own, or when you click Restore.

What it does

Restores on its own

After a display connects or disconnects. Or by hand from the window, the menu bar or a shortcut.

Several layouts per monitor set

Keep “Work”, “Evening” and “Presentation” for the same desk. Switch with ⌃⌥1 to 9; a banner names the one that is on.

Preview before you switch

Click another layout to see it without touching the screen. Colors show what would change.

Reopens missing apps

Restore with Missing Apps launches the closed apps of a layout first, then puts every Space in place.

Never quits your apps

Apps outside the layout can stay or be hidden: hiding only takes them out of full screen and minimizes them.

Works from the keyboard

Arrow keys walk layouts and monitors in the window, Return jumps to a Space. English, Polish, Spanish and Chinese.

macOS forgets where your Spaces go.
SpaceDock remembers, for every desk you sit at.

Every monitor set, with its layouts

The set that is connected now has a tick. The window shows its Spaces display by display, in the saved order.

See the difference first

Blue is open now but not in the layout. Red is in the layout but not open. Nothing moves until you click Restore.

Set it up once

The first launch needs one extra click, because SpaceDock is not notarized by Apple. Updates come inside the app after that.

  1. Arrange your Spaces

    Put full screen apps and desktops in the order you like on the monitors you have now.

  2. Save the layout

    Click Save Current Layout and give it a name. Repeat for every monitor set you use.

  3. Plug and unplug as usual

    SpaceDock puts the Spaces back when the displays settle.

Questions

How do I keep my Spaces in order when I connect an external monitor?

Save a layout in SpaceDock for each monitor set. When you connect or disconnect a display, SpaceDock restores the saved order of Spaces and full screen apps on every screen.

How do I open SpaceDock the first time?

Notarization needs a paid Apple Developer Program membership, and SpaceDock is free, so macOS blocks the first launch. Open System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down and click Open Anyway. Or run this in Terminal:

xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/SpaceDock.app

Updates after that install without the prompt.

Why does it need the Accessibility permission?

To read which window is on which Space and to move windows in and out of full screen. Nothing leaves your Mac.

Does it use private APIs?

Yes: SkyLight, the framework macOS itself uses for Spaces. It needs no changes to System Integrity Protection. A macOS update can break these calls; SpaceDock then falls back to slower restore methods.

Should I turn off “Automatically rearrange Spaces based on most recent use”?

Yes, in System Settings → Desktop & Dock → Mission Control. With it on, macOS reorders Spaces every time you switch, so the saved order drifts even without a monitor change.

Does it quit or close my apps?

Never. Hiding apps outside the layout only takes them out of full screen and minimizes them. Restore with Missing Apps opens closed apps, but they start fresh: SpaceDock does not bring back what was open inside them, such as browser tabs.

What does it send over the network?

Only the daily update check, which downloads appcast.xml from the latest GitHub release; you can turn it off in Settings. Layouts stay in ~/Library/Application Support/SpaceDock and contain app names and window titles.

How do I uninstall it?

Quit SpaceDock and delete the app. Optionally remove ~/Library/Application Support/SpaceDock, ~/Library/Logs/SpaceDock.log and the permission:

tccutil reset Accessibility io.github.wiciow.spacedock
Can I use it at work?

For noncommercial use, yes. SpaceDock is source-available under the PolyForm Noncommercial license; commercial use needs the author's permission, so open an issue on GitHub.
